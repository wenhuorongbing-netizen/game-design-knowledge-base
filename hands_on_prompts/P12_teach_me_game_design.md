# P12 - Teach Me Game Design

## Use Case

Use this when you want to learn a game design concept or domain.

## Copy-Paste Prompt

> Use the Game Design Knowledgebase as an AI Game Design Master runtime. Teach me this game design topic as a draft/source-governed lesson.
>
> Topic: [paste topic]
>
> My level: [beginner, intermediate, advanced, or unknown]
>
> Safety and behavior rules: do not invent facts, player reactions, market data, project details, evidence, citations, quotes, user notes, legal sidecars, playtest results, telemetry, or benchmark outputs. Label assumptions, `source_basis`, and confidence. Ask at most 3 high-value questions if needed. Produce a concrete mini lesson and exercise. Do not cite books unless evidence is available. Do not claim verified status without EvidenceRef and review.

## What To Replace

- Replace `[paste topic]` with a concept, domain, or problem.
- Replace `[beginner, intermediate, advanced, or unknown]` with your level.

## What AI Should Produce

- Working explanation.
- Why it matters.
- When to use it.
- When not to use it.
- Mini exercise.
- Next concept.
- Evidence needed for stronger claims.

## Output Format

| Section | Lesson |
|---|---|
| working definition | pending |
| why it matters | pending |
| use it when | pending |
| avoid it when | pending |
| exercise | pending |
| next topic | pending |

Footer must include `source_basis`, confidence, assumptions, evidence gaps, and next action.

## Source And Confidence Rules

Default `source_basis`: `unsupported_draft`.

Default confidence: `weak`.

Teaching explanations are draft unless supported by EvidenceRefs.

## No Fake Evidence Rule

Do not pretend to summarize a book chapter or cite a source without evidence.

## Follow-Up Prompt

> Give me a practice exercise and then review my answer using the same source/confidence rules.

## Self-Check Prompt

> Check whether the lesson is understandable, practical, and source-safe. Remove unsupported book claims.
