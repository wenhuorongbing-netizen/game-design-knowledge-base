# Book Reading Sequence

Date: 2026-04-29

## Purpose

This reading sequence connects books to AI master capabilities. It is not a chapter summary and does not require the AI to parse private source bodies.

## Reading By Capability

| Capability | Priority Works | Why Read Them Here | User Notes To Capture | AI Must Not Claim Yet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core Experience Master | The Art of Game Design; A Theory of Fun; Game Design Workshop | Helps frame player experience, design intent, and early concept direction. | What experience goal matters? What fantasy or emotion should the AI preserve? | Exact book definitions, lens wording, or chapter arguments. |
| Lens Review Master | The Art of Game Design; Advanced Game Design; Game Design Workshop | Supports diagnostic questioning and design review habits. | Which question changed how you evaluate a game? How would you rewrite it as an original KB lens? | Proprietary lens text or book-specific lens wording. |
| Rules and Mechanics Master | Rules of Play; Game Mechanics; The Game Design Reader | Supports rules, objectives, mechanics, procedures, and formal structure. | What formal element did you notice? What design artifact should the AI produce? | Source-backed definitions without evidence. |
| Meaningful Decision Master | Game Mechanics; Formal Abstract Design Tools; Characteristics of Games | Supports tradeoffs, dilemmas, chance, skill, and decision quality. | What makes a decision meaningful in your interpretation? What example can you describe in your own words? | Claims that the book states a universal rule. |
| Systems and Economy Master | Advanced Game Design; Game Mechanics; Characteristics of Games | Supports loops, feedback, emergence, sources, sinks, balance, and progression. | What loop or economy pattern should the AI recognize? What risk should it check? | Specific diagrams, chapter models, or body-derived methods. |
| Play and Player Experience Master | Play Matters; The Aesthetic of Play; A Theory of Fun | Supports play, playfulness, motivation, learning, mastery, and experience. | What does this change about how you see play? What question should AI ask designers? | Exact philosophical claims without notes/evidence. |
| Game Feel and Feedback Master | Game Feel; Level Up | Supports real-time control, response, feedback, polish, and interface feel. | What feel variable did you notice? What should the AI ask when diagnosing feel? | Exact metrics or terminology as book-derived without evidence. |
| UI/UX Feedback Master | Level Up; Game Feel; Better Game Characters by Design | Supports readability, feedback, player state, interface, and accessibility. | What UI/feedback failure did you observe? What test would reveal it? | Claims about accessibility or player comprehension without data. |
| Narrative-System Integration Master | Better Game Characters by Design; Level Up; Chris Crawford works | Supports character function, avatar identity, narrative agency, and mechanic alignment. | What narrative-system relationship should the AI check? | Source-specific narrative theory without evidence. |
| Prototyping and Playtesting Master | Game Design Workshop; Challenges for Game Designers; The Art of Game Design | Supports prototypes, exercises, playtests, observation, and iteration. | What process step should become a workflow? What observation rule matters? | Specific exercises copied from books. |
| Production and Pitch Master | Level Up; Game Design Workshop; Chris Crawford works | Supports documentation, pitch, feasibility, production, and release readiness. | What pitch structure or scope rule should the AI remember? | Market certainty or production claims without context. |
| Community and Ethics Master | Play Matters; Bartle player types; Better Game Characters by Design | Supports player rights, community health, multiplayer behavior, ethics, and responsibility. | What risk should AI flag? What user group might be affected? | Legal advice or universal ethical verdicts. |

## Reading Note Rule

For every manual reading pass, create at least one user-authored note with:

- core idea in your words;
- design problem it helps solve;
- lens or question it suggests;
- workflow it could improve;
- example you thought of;
- what AI must not claim yet.
