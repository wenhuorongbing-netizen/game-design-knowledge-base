Artifact title: Meaningful Decision Audit - Attack, Defend, Wait

User goal: Diagnose why players always attack and identify repairs that make combat choices meaningful.

Assumptions: "Players always attack" is user-observed or user-suspected, but no playtest log, numerical rules, enemy behavior, damage values, or turn order were supplied. I will not treat it as verified playtest evidence.

Decision point: On a combat turn, the player chooses attack, defend, or wait.

Player options:

| Option | Apparent value | Likely problem |
|---|---|---|
| Attack | Progresses toward victory immediately | Dominates if damage is always useful and risk is low |
| Defend | Reduces incoming harm | Weak if incoming attacks are unpredictable or prevention is less valuable than ending fight |
| Wait | Delays action | Weak if waiting does not change information, timing, positioning, or resource state |

Player information: Unknown. The player may not know enemy intent, damage ranges, future turn order, defense value, or wait benefit.

Consequences: Currently unclear, but the observed pattern suggests attack has the best expected value or the clearest perceived value.

Failure mode: Likely obvious decision. It may also be a fake choice if defend and wait rarely create better outcomes than attack.

Repair options:

- Give defend a timing reason: show enemy intent, then make defend powerful against specific incoming attacks.
- Give wait an information or tempo reason: waiting could reveal enemy intent, charge a counter, improve position, or alter turn order.
- Add attack risk: attacking could expose the player, consume stamina, trigger retaliation, or prevent defensive preparation.
- Create enemy states: armored, charging, vulnerable, distracted, unstable. Each state should make a different option attractive.
- Add short-term vs long-term tradeoff: attack wins now, defend preserves future resources, wait sets up a stronger next turn.

Prototype test: Create one enemy with three visible intents: strike, charge, and guard. Run three turns where attack, defend, and wait each becomes best once. Check whether a player can explain why.

Artifact title: Prototype Plan - Resource Loop Comprehension

User goal: Build the smallest prototype that tests whether players understand a resource loop in a small strategy game.

Assumptions: The resource loop is not yet specified. I assume the loop has at least one source, one player spend decision, one sink, and one feedback signal. No engine, platform, team size, timebox, or actual project facts were supplied.

Prototype question: Can a player understand what creates the resource, what spends it, and why the loop matters after three turns?

Hypothesis: If the source, sink, and feedback are visible in the same turn cycle, players can predict the result of spending or saving the resource by turn three.

Minimum build scope:

- One resource, such as energy, food, coins, or influence.
- One source action that creates the resource.
- One spend action that consumes it.
- One sink or upkeep cost that removes it.
- One visible consequence of shortage or surplus.
- Three to five turns.
- One page of rules or a simple spreadsheet/paper board.

Excluded features:

- full economy;
- multiple currencies;
- upgrades;
- AI opponent;
- long-term progression;
- narrative content;
- balance tuning beyond clarity.

Success signal: After the third turn, the player can answer: "Where did the resource come from, where did it go, and what should I do next?"

Failure signal: The player spends randomly, cannot predict the next resource amount, or cannot identify the sink.

Timebox: 2 to 4 hours for a paper or spreadsheet prototype.

Next decision: If players understand the loop, test whether it creates interesting tradeoffs. If they do not, simplify the resource display or reduce the number of transformations.
